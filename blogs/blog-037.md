Steven Wright said: "I'm actually writing a short story about a photographer who went completely insane trying to take a close up photo of the horizon." In Product Management, we often do the same thing.

We go crazy trying to reach a "Final" version that doesn't exist. Whether it’s a PRD, a slide deck, or a roadmap, we are all just one "Save As" away from a mirage.

In a previous post, I talked about being in Draft Mode: the idea that you aren’t stuck, you’re just mid-edit. But if life is a draft, we have to talk about the destination we’re all sprinting toward: the "Final" version. 

We’ve all seen the file on the shared drive: Project_Alpha_Spec_FINAL.docx. Then Project_Alpha_Spec_FINAL_v2.docx. Then, in a moment of desperate optimism, Project_Alpha_Spec_STAKEHOLDER_APPROVED_FINAL.docx.

We are obsessed with reaching the end of the document, but the truth is that the "Final" version is a mirage. The moment you think you’ve reached it, the market moves.

### The Artifact vs. The Evolution
In the corporate world, we are trained to worship the Artifact. We want the shiny, shrink-wrapped launch. We want the "Project Closed" email. But the most successful products I’ve ever touched were the ones that never actually finished evolving. They were living organisms that responded to a shift in the market, a bug in the code, or a change in user behavior.

I spent nearly 3 years delivering a digital transformation project at Wells Fargo, and then spent the next 5 years improving on what I delivered. The end was never defined, just the end of this part of the continuum of the project. 

### The Archaeology of the "Unfinished"
There is a specific kind of noir-tinged anxiety that comes with the "Unfinished." It’s the feeling that if a feature isn't perfect, it’s a failure. But look closer at the "Archaeology" of any great company. Underneath the polished UI of a billion-dollar app, you’ll find layers of legacy code and "temporary" hotfixes that have survived for a decade.

The "Final" version is just a snapshot of the best you could do with the time and tools you had. In PM work, the "Final" version is simply the point where you stopped typing and started shipping.

### Learning to Love Version 0.8
If we accept that "Final" is a myth, we can stop holding our breath:

- Product Strategy: The roadmap isn't a carved stone tablet; it’s a living document that should change when we learn something new.
- The PRD: It isn't an artifact to be archived; it's the starting line for a conversation with engineering.
- The Launch: It's not the end of the journey; it's the first time you're getting real data.
### 
Stop waiting for the mirage to turn into solid ground. Embrace the draft. Ship the Version 0.8. The world doesn't need "Final." It needs what you have to offer right now. You can always edit the "Final_V3" tomorrow.
