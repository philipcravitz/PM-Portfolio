Hey Product People! You don't need a HAM radio license to find your true user signal, but you do need a filter, because the broadcast “Absolute Panic” is coming in too loud and too clear.

You're staring at a giant, unruly ocean of feedback. It’s not just a steady stream; it’s a chaotic, crashing wave of support tickets, survey responses, sales requests, forum posts, and LinkedIn DMs. Everyone is shouting, and every shout feels urgent.

The core problem isn't a lack of information; it’s the Noise.

We collect, categorize, and count every piece of input, seeking the golden thread, the Signal, that points to true, unmet user need. Instead, we end up optimizing for volume, prioritizing features requested by the loudest (or wealthiest) customer, or bugs reported most frequently this week.

In both cases, we've confused frequency with foundational value.

The loudest input often represents one of two things:

The High-Volume Noise (The Trivial Many): A minor usability friction point that affects many people but causes minimal pain (e.g., "The button is blue, I wish it were green"). Building this provides a quick dopamine hit, but offers zero strategic value.

The High-Value Minority Noise (The Tactical Necessity): A request from a single, critical customer or an internal stakeholder who controls a budget. While sometimes a tactical necessity, it can quickly steer the product’s architecture into a personalized alleyway, compromising the main highway for all other users.

A great PM doesn't just synthesize feedback; they systematically ignore everything that isn't the Signal.

The Signal is the data that illuminates a core, unmet user pain point that aligns with your long-term product vision. It's the reason why users are really using your product.

### Three Filters to Silence the Shouting

The Problem-Fit Filter: Does this feedback identify a fundamental pain point, or is it just prescribing a solution? (e.g., The Signal: "I can't share files securely." The Noise: "I need a dedicated green 'share' button.") Rule: Always ruthlessly discard prescribed solutions until you understand the underlying pain.

The Frequency-Impact Filter: You must weigh frequency against severity. A critical, recurring error reported by a small, high-value segment is a louder Signal than a cosmetic bug reported by a thousand users. Use a simple matrix to score based on Impact x Severity.

The Vision Filter: If the feedback addresses a pain point, but is outside the scope of your core product mission, it's irrelevant Noise.

Remember, our job is to listen to what users are saying, yes, but also we need to dig for why they are saying it. Only the strongest signals should make it onto the core roadmap, the rest is just wind and waves.
