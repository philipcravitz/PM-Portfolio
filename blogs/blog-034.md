“What are you looking for in a job?” This simple question, asked many times over through my Interview experiences, has forced me to look inside and start being real with myself about what I want.

Earlier in this process, my answer to this question would shift slightly depending on the room. I was trying to find the right frequency for the listener. But over time, something shifted. No matter who was asking the question, my answer stopped changing. The fluff fell away, and the core truth clarified.

I realized my professional energy isn't tied to a title or a trendy tech stack. It’s tied to a very specific set of conditions. If I can find the intersection of these three pillars, I won’t just "go to work." I’ll jump out of bed, ready to dive in.

### 1. Meaningful Impact: From Mission to Stewardship

I am no longer looking for just a "mission." I am looking for stewardship. It is one thing to optimize a conversion funnel for a product people want; it is another to be the steward of a product people need. I want to work on something that matters in a dinner table conversation: health, financial security, and the infrastructure of a person's future.

When you act as a steward, the stakes change. You aren't just shipping features; you are managing the trust of a human being. I am at my best when I feel the weight of that responsibility. I want the role where the cost of a "bad product decision" isn't just a dip in a graph, but a tangible setback in a user’s life. If the work doesn't carry that weight, it eventually feels hollow.

### 2. Scale and the Rigor of Consequence

Impact is the foundation, but scale is the crucible.

I am driven by the specific rigor required to move the needle for millions. At that altitude, you cannot rely on "hacks" or manual workarounds. You have to build systems that are resilient and inclusive by default. At scale, a "small" edge case is actually thousands of people in crisis.

I am looking for a role where the consequence of scale forces a higher level of craftsmanship. I am interested in the problems where you refuse to compromise on architectural integrity because you know that "tech debt" at scale eventually becomes a tax on the user experience. I want to build the "ripple" that improves as many lives as possible.

### 3. The Puzzle-Solver’s High: The Clarity of Negative Space

I love to be challenged, but I’ve realized that I am specifically bored by "predictable" growth. I am not looking for a role where the path is paved and the "how" is a foregone conclusion.

I am looking for ambiguity and constraints. I thrive on the messy architectural knots and the creative workarounds required when technical limitations meet high user expectations.

What excites me is the "hard thing" that hasn't been solved yet: the puzzle that requires a solution that is both elegant and interesting. I want the role where the primary task is to find a path through a fog of conflicting personas and legacy systems. If the answer is already in the playbook, I’m likely the wrong person for the job.

### The Alignment of Conviction
When these three pillars align: stewardship, scale, and high-ambiguity puzzles: work stops being a series of tasks and starts being a source of energy.

I am not looking for a place to simply "land." I am looking for the "hard puzzles" that serve the "many." There is a specific kind of satisfaction that comes from solving a complex problem that actually makes the world a bit easier to navigate for a lot of people.

That is the work that makes me rush to the desk every morning.
