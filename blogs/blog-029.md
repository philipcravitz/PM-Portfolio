The lesson of Scylla and Charybdis was never about finding a perfect, obstacle-free route: it was about knowing your direction when the plan collapses in real time. It is the art of the pivot.

Every product roadmap wants to be a set of tracks: fixed, predictable, and reassuring. But building a product is not building a railroad. It is seamanship.

A roadmap is drawn while the ship is still in the harbor. The moment you push off, the sea starts arguing with your assumptions. The weather turns. Currents pull sideways. If you cling too tightly to the original coordinates, you do not stay on course. You simply drift while falling behind schedule.

This is the fatal error: confusing the roadmap for the North Star.

Odysseus did not survive by following a pristine map. He survived by navigating the narrow strait between Scylla and Charybdis as the moment demanded. The danger was unavoidable. The choice was not whether to change course, but how deliberately to do so without losing sight of home. Effective strategy is not a static document; it is a live performance. That tension is where real leadership lives.

### When the Sea Speaks
I saw this clearly during a multi-year digital redesign. We had a well-socialized roadmap and a set of "confident" call-to-action (CTA) designs. They were clean, elegant, and internally celebrated.

Then reality arrived.

The CTAs were not just missing their KPIs; they were off by 50%. The sea had spoken. We had two choices: defend the plan or adjust the course.

We chose the harder path. We treated the data like a shift in the wind, not a failure of execution. That decision forced a reset. We reworked "finished" designs, overhauled value messaging, and reconsidered which contact methods to highlight. We prioritized the user over our egos.

The roadmap bent. The destination did not.

### Discipline vs. Stubbornness
Many teams resist this level of pivot. Adjusting the plan feels like admitting you were "wrong." In reality, it is admitting you are paying attention. A captain who refuses to tack into the wind is not disciplined. They are stubborn.

Roadmaps are tactical artifacts. They should change as frequently as the weather. When they do not, teams sail straight while the current quietly carries them toward the rocks.

### Sacred Direction, Flexible Plans
The North Star is different. It is a guiding principle: a promise about the outcome you are committed to creating. It tells you where North is, not which sail to raise today.

The healthiest product teams I have worked with treat roadmaps as flexible and direction as sacred. They revisit plans constantly and purpose rarely. That is not chaos. That is seamanship.

Roadmaps that never change are not signs of certainty. They are signs of denial. The North Star is what gets you home. Everything else is just how you try to get there today.
